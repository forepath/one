Remote access that follows your policies

People, machines and whole offices connect to the cluster over WireGuard and reach internal services according to the policies assigned to them, so a full company can run on Loadweaver.

Request early access The policy model

Users, machines and offices

Every client belongs to groups in the same policy model as your workloads. Policies stay allow-only: a client sees nothing unless a policy allows it.

People

A client is a device that belongs to a user. Identity-provider groups map to client groups, so "sales may reach the CRM on 443" is just a policy.

Machines

CI runners, servers elsewhere and office routers connect as machine clients with their own group membership, enrolled by admins.

Offices

Any WireGuard-capable router or firewall, such as OPNsense, pfSense, MikroTik, Fritz!Box or Linux, announces its office subnets. They become a group; overlapping subnets are rejected in the plan.

One endpoint, short-lived keys, routes from policies

Clients never need to know which gateway they use, keys do not live forever, and every client only gets the routes its policies allow.

Gateways without a single point of failure

Nodes in a vpn group act as gateways. Clients get one endpoint hostname that resolves via failover IP, anycast or DNS failover, and a client's key is valid on all gateways. A gateway failure means a short reconnect, not a reconfiguration, and a single gateway shows up as a pin.

Self-service enrollment

In the console, desktop app or CLI. The keypair is generated on the device where possible, so the private key never leaves it.

Keys that expire

For example after 30 days, renewed after a fresh sign-in with MFA, since WireGuard itself has no login.

Instant revocation

A revoked device is gone from all gateways at once, and offboarding a user in Keycloak revokes all their devices.

Split tunnel by default

Only the prefixes and service IPs a client's policies allow are routed, and internal names resolve through the policy-aware cluster DNS.

Full tunnel where needed

Groups that need it send all their traffic through the cluster, past the egress proxy and egress policies.

Device posture as a policy condition

"Finance needs an encrypted disk, an OS patched within 30 days and a low EDR risk" is a condition on a client group.

A device that fails its posture moves into a restricted group instead of being disconnected, for example with only the helpdesk and the wiki in reach, and the user sees why.

Core checks

Disk encryption, OS version and patch level, screen lock, OS firewall and app version, reported at connect and periodically.

MDM

Compliance state from Microsoft Intune, Jamf and Kandji, behind a posture plugin interface.

EDR

Risk level from Microsoft Defender for Endpoint, CrowdStrike and SentinelOne.

Device certificates

From your company CA, or from Loadweaver's own CA, issued at enrollment.

Sessions under control

Alerts
